# Chapter 2.2: Equity Fund Categories

> **Core**

> **What you'll learn:** why SEBI standardised fund categories in 2017, every equity fund category and its rules, how they differ in risk and return, which categories are useful for a beginner, and which to be wary of.

---

## 1. Why categories matter

Before October 2017, AMCs could launch any number of funds with vague names ("Opportunities", "Growth", "Prudence"). Similar-sounding funds held very different things, and comparing them was close to impossible.

**SEBI's 2017 circular on Categorisation and Rationalisation of Mutual Fund Schemes**:
- defined **36 standard categories** across equity, debt, hybrid, solution-oriented and others
- set **clear investment rules** for each (e.g. how much must be in large caps)
- allowed **only one scheme per category per AMC** (index funds, ETFs, FoFs and sectoral/thematic funds are exceptions)
- forced AMCs to merge or reclassify hundreds of overlapping schemes

### The 2026 update (SEBI circular of 26 February 2026)
- **Value and contra:** an AMC may now offer **both** a value fund and a contra fund, as long as their portfolios overlap by **no more than 50%** (AMCs have 3 years to comply).
- **Sectoral and thematic** funds are now **two separate categories**.
- **New: life cycle funds.** A target-date style category: you pick a fund matching your goal year, and it follows a set glide path, shifting from equity to debt as that year approaches (the idea in Chapter 3.2, done for you).
- **Solution-oriented funds** (children's and retirement funds) are **discontinued**: no fresh subscriptions, and existing schemes will be merged into other categories.

**Why this helps you:** you can now compare funds **within the same category** fairly. Comparing a small-cap fund's returns with a large-cap fund's is meaningless. They carry completely different risks.

### The definition of large, mid and small cap
Recall (Chapter 1.5):
- **Large cap:** companies ranked **1–100** by full market cap
- **Mid cap:** ranked **101–250**
- **Small cap:** ranked **251 onwards**

AMFI publishes the official list every six months.

## 2. Equity fund categories (SEBI rules)

All equity schemes must have **at least 65% in equity** (except where a higher limit is stated).

| # | Category | SEBI investment rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Large Cap Fund** | ≥ **80%** in large caps | Invests in India's top 100 companies. Most stable equity category. |
| 2 | **Large & Mid Cap Fund** | ≥ **35%** large caps AND ≥ **35%** mid caps | A blend of stability and growth |
| 3 | **Mid Cap Fund** | ≥ **65%** in mid caps | Higher growth potential, higher volatility |
| 4 | **Small Cap Fund** | ≥ **65%** in small caps | Highest risk and return potential in diversified equity. Can fall 50–60% in bad years. |
| 5 | **Multi Cap Fund** | ≥ **25%** each in large, mid AND small caps (≥75% equity) | A forced allocation to all three sizes. Created in 2020. |
| 6 | **Flexi Cap Fund** | ≥ **65%** equity, **any mix** of sizes | The manager has full freedom to shift between sizes. One of the most popular categories. |
| 7 | **Focused Fund** | Max **30 stocks** | Concentrated portfolio. Outcomes depend heavily on the manager. |
| 8 | **Value Fund** | Follows a value strategy | Buys stocks that look cheap on valuation metrics |
| 9 | **Contra Fund** | Follows a contrarian strategy | Buys out-of-favour stocks. (Since February 2026, an AMC can run both a value and a contra fund if their portfolios overlap by at most 50%.) |
| 10 | **Dividend Yield Fund** | Mainly invests in dividend-yielding stocks | Tends toward mature, cash-rich companies |
| 11 | **Sectoral Fund** | ≥ **80%** in one sector | Banking, IT, pharma… |
| 12 | **Thematic Fund** | ≥ **80%** in one theme | Infrastructure, PSU, consumption, manufacturing, ESG… (a separate category from sectoral since February 2026) |
| 13 | **ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)** | ≥ **80%** in equity, **3-year lock-in** | Tax deduction under 80C (old regime only) |

Plus **index funds and ETFs** (passive), which can track any index: Nifty 50, Next 50, Midcap 150, Nifty 500, etc.

## 3. Understanding each major category

### 3.1 Large cap funds
- **Holds:** HDFC Bank, Reliance, ICICI Bank, Infosys, TCS, Bharti Airtel, L&T, ITC… the same stocks that dominate the Nifty 50.
- **Volatility:** lowest among equity categories. Falls are usually smaller than in mid and small caps.
- **The big problem:** since these funds must hold at least 80% in the top 100 stocks, and these are the most researched stocks in India, **it's very hard for an active manager to beat the index**. Most don't, after costs (Chapter 2.4).
- **Verdict:** use a **Nifty 50 or Nifty 100 index fund** instead of an active large-cap fund.

### 3.2 Mid cap funds
- **Holds:** companies ranked 101–250. Often established businesses that could become future large caps.
- **Behavior:** can outperform large caps strongly in bull markets and fall harder in bear markets. Mid caps fell much more than the Nifty 50 in 2008 and 2018–2020.
- **Verdict:** a reasonable "satellite" for investors with a 7–10+ year horizon. Index option: **Nifty Midcap 150 index fund**.

### 3.3 Small cap funds
- **Holds:** companies ranked 251+. Many are small, less researched and less liquid.
- **Behavior:** extreme swings. Falls of 50–70% have happened (2008, 2018–2020). Rallies can be equally dramatic.
- **Liquidity issue:** when everyone redeems at once, small-cap funds struggle to sell holdings without pushing prices down. In 2024, SEBI asked AMCs to run and disclose **stress tests** showing how many days it would take to liquidate 25% or 50% of the portfolio.
- **Verdict:** only for those with **10+ year horizons and a strong stomach**. Keep it a small portion (0–15% of equity).

### 3.4 Flexi cap funds
- **The manager decides** the mix of large, mid and small caps based on where they see value.
- Most flexi cap funds in practice are 60–75% large cap.
- Some include international stocks (e.g. Parag Parikh Flexi Cap historically held US stocks).
- **Verdict:** the most sensible **single active fund** if you want one, because the manager isn't boxed into one segment. It's still subject to manager risk.

### 3.5 Multi cap funds
- Created in 2020, requiring at least 25% each in large, mid and small caps.
- The **forced 25% small-cap exposure** makes them more volatile than flexi cap funds.
- **Verdict:** fine, but understand you're getting meaningful small-cap exposure.

### 3.6 Large & mid cap funds
- At least 35% each in large and mid caps. A middle path.
- **Verdict:** can be replicated with a Nifty 50 index fund plus a Midcap 150 index fund.

### 3.7 Focused funds
- Max 30 stocks, high conviction. The result depends heavily on the manager's skill and luck.
- **Verdict:** higher manager risk. Not necessary for beginners.

### 3.8 Value, contra and dividend yield funds
- Style-based funds. Value and contra funds can underperform for years when "growth" stocks are in favour, then outperform sharply.
- **Verdict:** only if you understand and believe in the style, and can sit through long dry spells.

### 3.9 Sectoral and thematic funds
- **Sectoral funds** bet on one sector (banking, IT, pharma). **Thematic funds** bet on a theme that cuts across sectors (infra, PSU, consumption, manufacturing, defence, EV, ESG, "Make in India"). Since February 2026, SEBI treats them as two separate categories, but the risks below apply to both.
- **Problems:**
  - **Concentration:** if the sector has a bad decade, the fund does too. IT funds were flat for long periods. Infra funds lost heavily after 2008 and took a decade to recover.
  - **Marketing timing:** AMCs launch thematic NFOs when the theme is **hot**, after it has already risen a lot. Investors pile in at the top.
  - Investors usually **earn less than the fund's own reported returns**, because they buy after rallies and sell after falls.
- **Verdict:** **avoid as a core holding.** At most, a small "satellite" for someone with genuine sector knowledge.

### 3.10 ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)
- An equity fund (≥80% equity, usually flexi-cap-like) with a **3-year lock-in**.
- Gives a deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh under **Section 80C**, but **only in the old tax regime**.
- The **shortest lock-in among 80C options** (PPF 15 years, tax-saver FD 5 years, NSC 5 years).
- **Each SIP instalment is locked for 3 years from its own date.** An SIP started in January 2026 means the January 2026 instalment unlocks in January 2029, the February instalment in February 2029, and so on.
- **Verdict:** if you're in the old regime and need 80C, ELSS is the best equity-oriented option. In the new regime, it's just an equity fund with an unnecessary lock-in.

## 4. Index fund categories (passive equity)

| Index fund type | What it holds | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Nifty 50 / Sensex** | Top 50 / 30 companies | **Core holding** for most investors |
| **Nifty Next 50** | Companies ranked ~51–100 | Adds "future large caps". More volatile than the Nifty 50. |
| **Nifty 100 / Nifty 500 / Total Market** | Broader market | One-fund coverage of large (and mid/small) caps |
| **Nifty Midcap 150** | Mid caps | Low-cost mid-cap exposure |
| **Nifty Smallcap 250** | Small caps | Low-cost small-cap exposure |
| **Smart beta / factor** (Momentum, Low Volatility, Quality, Alpha, Value, Equal Weight) | Rule-based stock selection | Advanced. Can underperform for long periods. Skip initially. |
| **International** (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100 via FoF) | US companies | Geographic diversification. Subject to overseas investment limits. |

## 5. Risk ladder of equity categories

```
Lower risk                                                     Higher risk
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────►
Large Cap / Nifty 50 → Flexi Cap → Large & Mid → Multi Cap → Mid Cap → Small Cap → Sectoral/Thematic
```

SEBI's **Riskometer** on every fund page classifies funds into six levels: Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High, **Very High**. Almost all equity funds are "Very High". That label refers to volatility, not the chance of permanent loss in a diversified fund held long-term.

## 6. How many equity funds do you need?

**Two to four, at most.** More funds usually means:
- **Overlap:** five large-cap and flexi-cap funds will hold mostly the same 30–40 stocks.
- **Hidden index-hugging:** a portfolio of many active funds behaves like an expensive index fund.
- **Harder tracking and rebalancing.**

### Sample sensible equity setups
| Investor | Equity funds |
|---|---|
| Simplest | 1 fund: **Nifty 50 index fund** (or Nifty 500 / total market index) |
| Beginner+ | Nifty 50 index (70%) + Nifty Next 50 index or Midcap 150 index (30%) |
| One active fund | Nifty 50 index (60–70%) + one flexi-cap fund (30–40%) |
| Wants global exposure | Any of the above + 10–20% in an S&P 500 / global FoF |

Use an **overlap tool** (Value Research, Morningstar, or third-party tools) to check how much two funds share.

## 7. Summary

- SEBI's 2017 categorisation lets you compare funds **within** a category. Always compare like with like.
- Large cap = top 100, mid = 101–250, small = 251+.
- **Large cap:** most active funds lag the index (74–85% depending on the period, Chapter 2.4), so use an index fund.
- **2026 changes:** value and contra funds can now coexist (max 50% overlap), sectoral and thematic are separate categories, life cycle (target-date) funds are new, and solution-oriented funds are being discontinued.
- **Flexi cap:** a sensible single active fund. **Mid and small caps:** long horizons and small allocations only.
- **Sectoral and thematic:** avoid as core holdings. They're usually marketed at peaks.
- **ELSS:** only if you're in the old regime and need 80C. Each SIP instalment has its own 3-year lock-in.
- Hold **2–4 funds** at most. Check overlap.
