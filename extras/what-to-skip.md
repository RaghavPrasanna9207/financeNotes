# What to Skip for Now (and Why)

> **Core**

> These topics take about **80% of the effort and give 20% of the value** at your stage. Each section explains what the topic is, why it's on the skip list, and when it might be worth learning later.

---

## 1. Technical analysis and chart patterns

**What it is:** studying **price charts and trading volumes** to predict short-term price moves. Tools include candlestick patterns (doji, hammer, engulfing), support and resistance, moving averages, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, Fibonacci levels, chart patterns (head and shoulders, triangles, flags).

**Why skip it:**
- It's a tool for **short-term trading**, not long-term investing. Your SIP into an index fund doesn't need a chart.
- Evidence that it delivers **consistent profits for retail traders after costs** is weak. Most short-term retail traders lose money.
- It encourages frequent action (Housel's Flaw #12) and treats markets as entertainment (Flaw #15).
- Frequent trading creates costs and short-term taxes (20% STCG on equity, or slab rates on business income from intraday trading).

**When to revisit:** only if you develop a genuine, disciplined interest in trading with a small, separate amount of "risk capital", and accept that most people who try it lose.

## 2. F&O (futures and options)

**What it is:** **derivatives** whose value depends on an underlying asset (a stock or index like the Nifty). Futures are obligations to buy or sell at a set price on a future date. Options give the right (but not the obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put). Both involve **leverage**: small capital controls large positions.

**Why skip it:**
- **SEBI's studies (2023 and 2024)** found that about **9 out of 10 individual F&O traders lost money**. Aggregate individual losses ran to more than ₹1 lakh crore over three years (FY22–FY24), and the average loss per person was over ₹1 lakh.
- **SEBI's FY25 study** found it got worse: **over 91%** of individual equity-derivative traders lost money, with net losses of about **₹1.06 lakh crore in FY25 alone**, up about **41%** from FY24.
- **Leverage** turns ordinary moves into large losses. That's exactly the "risk of ruin" Housel warns about (Flaw #16).
- Options lose value with time (theta decay). Weekly options in particular behave like lottery tickets.
- SEBI has tightened rules (higher contract sizes, fewer weekly expiries, upfront premium collection) specifically because of retail losses.
- F&O income is treated as **business income** (taxed at slab, needs ITR-3 and possibly an audit).

**When to revisit:** maybe never for speculation. Options can be used for **hedging** by experienced investors with large portfolios, which is an advanced topic for much later.

## 3. DCF (Discounted Cash Flow) valuation models

**What it is:** estimating a company's **intrinsic value** by forecasting its free cash flows for 10+ years, adding a "terminal value", and **discounting** everything back to today at a required rate of return (WACC). The result is compared with the current market price.

**Why skip it (for now):**
- It's **extremely sensitive to assumptions**. Changing the growth rate by 2% or the discount rate by 1% can move the "value" by 30–50%. Beginners can make any stock look cheap or expensive.
- It requires understanding the business deeply first, and that's what Week 4 (statements, ratios, moats, red flags) builds.
- Relative valuation (P/E vs history and peers, PEG, FCF yield) is enough at the intuition level.

**When to revisit:** if you start picking individual stocks seriously, after you've analysed 10–20 companies using the Week 4 framework. Varsity's Fundamental Analysis module has DCF chapters for later.

## 4. Macroeconomics

**What it is:** the study of the **whole economy**: GDP growth, inflation, interest rates (repo rate), fiscal deficit, current account deficit, currency movements, global events, government budgets, oil prices.

**Why skip it (as a basis for investment decisions):**
- It's interesting and useful general knowledge, but **nobody consistently predicts how macro events will affect markets**. Experts regularly get both the event *and* the market reaction wrong.
- Trying to time the market around budgets, elections, rate decisions or global news leads to **interrupting compounding**.
- Your **asset allocation** (equity + debt + gold, rebalanced annually) already handles macro uncertainty without predictions.
- You already know the bits that matter: inflation erodes cash (1.2), rates move bond prices (1.3), and the RBI targets 4% inflation.

**When to revisit:** read about it for general understanding at any time, but **don't trade on it**.

## 5. Crypto

**What it is:** digital assets such as Bitcoin, Ethereum and thousands of others, traded on crypto exchanges.

**Why skip it (or keep it tiny):**
- **No cash flows** (no earnings, interest or rent). Its value depends entirely on what the next buyer will pay.
- **Extreme volatility:** falls of 70–90% have happened several times.
- **Regulatory uncertainty in India:** it's not banned, but it isn't regulated as a securities or currency product either.
- **Harsh taxation in India:**
  - **Flat 30% tax** on gains (plus cess), regardless of holding period or income slab
  - **No set-off** of crypto losses against any other income or gains, not even other crypto gains
  - **1% TDS** on transfers above thresholds
  - No deductions except the cost of acquisition
- **Exchange and custody risks:** hacks, exchange collapses (e.g. FTX in 2022), lost keys.
- Many tokens are outright scams or pump-and-dumps.

**If you must:** limit it to a **tiny "fun money" slice** (say 1–2% of your net worth at most) that you can afford to lose **completely**, held only after your emergency fund, insurance and core SIPs are in place.

## 6. Other things you can safely ignore for now

| Topic | Why skip |
|---|---|
| **IPO flipping / listing gains** | A lottery. Allotments are random, and many hyped IPOs underperform after listing. |
| **Penny stocks / small-cap tips** | Prone to manipulation (pump and dump). Low liquidity. |
| **Stock tips from Telegram, WhatsApp, YouTube, "finfluencers"** | Often paid promotions or scams. SEBI has cracked down on unregistered advisers. |
| **PMS / AIFs** | Need ₹50 L / ₹1 Cr minimums. Fees are high. Not for beginners. |
| **Unlisted / pre-IPO shares** | Illiquid, opaque pricing, high risk |
| **Forex trading** | Leverage. Retail forex trading through unauthorised platforms is illegal in India (RBI's alert list). |
| **"Algo trading" courses and bots** | Usually expensive courses with little real edge |
| **Too many funds / portfolio tinkering** | 2–4 funds are enough (Chapter 2.2) |

## 7. What actually moves the needle

At your stage, these five things will determine **over 90% of your financial outcome**:

| Lever | Covered in |
|---|---|
| **1. Savings rate:** how much of your income you invest | 3.1 |
| **2. Time in the market:** starting early and never interrupting compounding | 1.1, 2.6 |
| **3. Asset allocation:** the right equity/debt/gold mix for each goal | 3.2 |
| **4. Low costs:** direct plans, index funds, tax efficiency | 2.4, 2.5, 3.5 |
| **5. Behavior:** not panicking, not chasing, staying the course | Psychology of Money |

Plus the **protection layer**: emergency fund, health and term insurance, and no high-interest debt (3.1, 3.7).

**Master these first.** Everything on the skip list can wait. Most of it can wait forever.
