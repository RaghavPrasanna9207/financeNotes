## 1. What is fundamental analysis?

**Fundamental analysis (FA)** is the process of evaluating a company's **business, financial health and future prospects** to judge whether it's a good business and whether its share price is reasonable relative to its **intrinsic value**.

The core belief: **in the long run, a stock's price follows the company's earnings and cash flows.** Short-term prices are driven by sentiment and news, but over 5–10+ years, businesses that grow profits steadily see their share prices follow.

### Fundamental vs technical analysis
| | Fundamental analysis | Technical analysis |
|---|---|---|
| Studies | The business: financials, management, industry, competitive position | Price charts, volumes, patterns, indicators |
| Question | "Is this a good business at a fair price?" | "Where is the price likely to go in the short term?" |
| Time horizon | Years | Days to weeks |
| Used by | Long-term investors | Traders |
| Tools | Annual reports, financial statements, ratios | Charts, moving averages, RSI |

These notes focus only on FA, at an intuition level (Week 4 of the plan). Technical analysis is on the "skip for now" list.

### Why learn FA even if you invest through index funds?
1. **You understand what you own.** An index fund owns businesses. FA tells you what makes them valuable.
2. **You can evaluate claims.** Tips, news and "hot stocks" become easier to judge.
3. **Better active fund evaluation:** you can read a fund's portfolio and see whether it's sensible.
4. **If you ever pick stocks** (a small satellite portion), you'll avoid the worst mistakes.

## 2. The investor's mindset

Varsity stresses that investing is about **owning businesses**, not trading tickers. Some principles:

1. **Think like a business owner.** If you couldn't sell the stock for 5 years, would you still be happy owning this business?
2. **A good company is not automatically a good investment.** Price matters. Paying 100× earnings for a great business can still give poor returns.
3. **Circle of competence (Warren Buffett).** Invest in businesses you understand. If you can't explain how the company makes money in two sentences, skip it.
4. **Long-term orientation.** Compounding needs time. Quality companies can compound at 15–20% for decades.
5. **Margin of safety (Benjamin Graham).** Buy at a price well below your estimate of value, to leave room for errors in your analysis.
6. **Accept that you'll be wrong sometimes.** That's why you diversify and size positions sensibly.

### Investible grade attributes
Varsity describes the kind of company a long-term investor looks for:
- **Consistent revenue and profit growth** over many years
- **Healthy, stable or improving margins**
- **High return on equity / capital** without excessive debt
- **Low or manageable debt**
- **Strong cash generation** (profits turning into actual cash)
- **A durable competitive advantage** (moat)
- **Honest, capable management** that treats minority shareholders fairly
- **Reasonable valuation**

## 3. Qualitative vs quantitative analysis

FA has two halves:

### Qualitative: the story (harder to measure)
- **Business model:** what the company sells, to whom, and how it makes money
- **Industry:** growth prospects, competition, regulation, cyclicality
- **Competitive advantage (moat):** brand, cost, network effects, switching costs (Chapter 4.6)
- **Management quality:** track record, integrity, capital allocation, communication
- **Corporate governance:** board independence, related-party transactions, auditor quality, treatment of minority shareholders
- **Promoter behavior:** holding, pledging, other businesses

### Quantitative: the numbers
- Financial statements: **P&L, balance sheet, cash flow** (Chapters 4.2–4.4)
- **Ratios:** growth, profitability, leverage, efficiency, valuation (Chapter 4.5)
- **Trends over 5–10 years**, and comparisons with peers

**Both matter.** Great numbers with dishonest management can collapse (Satyam). A great story with poor numbers is just a story.

## 4. The annual report: your primary source

Every listed company publishes an **annual report** each year (on its website, the BSE/NSE websites, and linked on Screener.in). It's the most comprehensive and reliable source of information about the company.

### What's inside (typical sections)
| Section | What you'll find | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Chairman's / MD's message** | Management's view of the year and future plans | Tone, honesty, strategy. Compare promises with what actually happened in previous years. |
| **Corporate information** | Board, auditors, bankers, registered office | Who the auditor is |
| **Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A)** | Industry overview, business segments, opportunities, threats, risks, outlook | **The most useful qualitative section.** Explains the business in management's words. |
| **Directors' report** | Performance summary, dividends, related-party transactions, subsidiaries, CSR | Governance details |
| **Corporate governance report** | Board composition, committee meetings, director remuneration | Independence, pay vs performance |
| **Business Responsibility & Sustainability Report (BRSR)** | ESG disclosures | Increasingly important |
| **Auditor's report** | The independent auditor's opinion on the financial statements | **Read it.** Look for "qualified opinion", "emphasis of matter" or "key audit matters". |
| **Financial statements (standalone)** | P&L, balance sheet, cash flow for the company alone | |
| **Financial statements (consolidated)** | Including subsidiaries, joint ventures and associates | **Usually the one to analyse**, as it shows the whole group |
| **Notes to accounts** | Details behind every number: accounting policies, debt breakdown, contingent liabilities, related parties | Where problems hide |

### Standalone vs consolidated
- **Standalone:** only the parent company's own numbers.
- **Consolidated:** parent + subsidiaries (combined as if one entity).
- Most investors analyse **consolidated** figures, since many companies operate through subsidiaries. Screener.in lets you toggle between the two.

### How to read an annual report (efficiently)
1. Read the **MD&A** and the **chairman's letter** to understand the business.
2. Skim the **auditor's report** for any qualification.
3. Look at the **three financial statements** (or use Screener's 10-year summary).
4. Check **notes** on: borrowings, contingent liabilities, related-party transactions, revenue recognition.
5. **Compare the last 3–5 years' chairman's letters:** did management do what it said it would?

## 5. Other sources of information

| Source | What it gives |
|---|---|
| **Quarterly results** (on exchange websites) | Latest P&L and segment data every quarter. The balance sheet is half-yearly. |
| **Investor presentations** | Management's slides: business segments, strategy, KPIs |
| **Earnings call (concall) transcripts** | Management answers analysts' questions. **Very revealing.** Listen for evasiveness, consistency and detail. |
| **Exchange filings / announcements** (BSE/NSE) | Board meetings, acquisitions, pledges, insider trades, credit rating changes |
| **Shareholding pattern** (quarterly) | Promoter, FII, DII and public holdings, pledges |
| **Credit rating reports** (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE) | Independent, detailed analysis of the business and its debt. Free and underrated. |
| **Screener.in, Tijori, Trendlyne, Tickertape** | 10-year financials, ratios, peer comparison (Chapter 4.8) |
| **Industry reports, news, product experience** | Context, and your own customer perspective |

## 7. Summary
- FA evaluates the **business** to judge its value. Technical analysis studies **price charts** (skip it for now).
- Mindset: **own businesses**, stay in your **circle of competence**, demand a **margin of safety**, think long-term.
- FA = **qualitative** (business, moat, management, governance) + **quantitative** (statements, ratios, trends).
- The **annual report** is the primary source. Read the **MD&A, auditor's report, statements and notes**. Use **consolidated** figures.
- Supplement it with concall transcripts, investor presentations, credit rating reports and Screener.in.
