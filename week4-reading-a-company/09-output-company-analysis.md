# Chapter 4.9: Week 4 Output: One-Page Analysis of a Company You Use

> **Core**

> **Goal:** analyse a listed company **whose product or service you actually use**, on a single page. Your experience as a customer gives you an edge in understanding the business. This pulls together everything from Chapters 4.1–4.8.

Allow 90–120 minutes: 30 minutes on Screener (Chapter 4.8 routine), 30–60 minutes reading a concall transcript and the annual report's MD&A, and 30 minutes writing.

## Worked example (abbreviated, using the fictional ABC Paints Ltd)

**1. What it does:** decorative paints manufacturer selling through ~50,000 dealers. About 85% decorative and 15% industrial coatings.

**2. As a customer:** my family repainted with ABC because the dealer recommended it and the colour-matching was good. Prices rose about 10% in 2 years and we still bought it.

**3. Numbers:** sales CAGR 13.6%, profit CAGR 15.8%, OPM 19–20% stable, ROE 20%, ROCE 25.5%, D/E 0.07 (net cash), interest coverage 37×, CFO/PAT 111%, FCF ₹700 Cr, CCC 63 days, no dilution. ✅ across the board.

**4. Valuation:** P/E 40× vs 5-year median ~55× (cheaper than its own history) but well above the Nifty (~21×). PEG 2.7. EV/EBITDA 27×. **Verdict: expensive in absolute terms, reasonable for its quality relative to its own history.**

**5. Moat:** brand + distribution (dealer and tinting network) + scale. Evidence: 10-year ROCE above 25%, stable margins through raw material swings. **Threat:** a well-funded conglomerate entering decorative paints could pressure margins and market share.

**6. Management:** promoter holding 52%, 0% pledged, stable. Delivered capacity additions as guided. Big-4 auditor, no qualifications. Small related-party transactions. Pays a 40% dividend and reinvests the rest.

**7. Red flags:** 0 of 9.

**8. Peers:** higher ROCE and a better balance sheet than peers. A P/E premium of about 15% over peers is justified by superior returns.

**9. Risks:** new well-funded competition; crude-linked raw material inflation; a slowdown in housing and repainting demand.

**10. Verdict:** **excellent business, fully priced.** It would be a good investment if it can sustain 14–15%+ earnings growth. I'd watch OPM (red flag if below 16% for 2+ years) and market share data in concalls. Better to accumulate slowly than to buy a large amount at once.
