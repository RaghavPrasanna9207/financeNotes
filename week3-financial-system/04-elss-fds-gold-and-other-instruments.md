# Chapter 3.4: ELSS, FDs, Small Savings Schemes, Gold and Other Instruments

> **What you'll learn:** ELSS in detail, how fixed deposits work (types, compounding, DICGC insurance, TDS, laddering), post office small savings schemes (NSC, SCSS, SSY, KVP, MIS, RD), RBI Floating Rate Bonds, all the ways to own gold and how they compare, and a master table of when each instrument makes sense.

> **Rates change quarterly** for small savings schemes and frequently for FDs. Figures are indicative (2025–26).

---

## 1. ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)

### Recap
- An **equity mutual fund** (≥80% in equity), usually managed like a flexi-cap fund.
- **3-year lock-in** from the date of each investment. **Each SIP instalment is locked separately.**
- **80C deduction up to ₹1.5 L** (combined with all other 80C items), **only in the old regime**.
- Gains are taxed as equity: **LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 L/year** (all ELSS redemptions are after 3 years, so always long-term).

### ELSS vs other 80C options
| Option | Lock-in | Return (indicative) | Tax on returns | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ELSS** | **3 years** | Market-linked (equity, ~11–13% long-run) | LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 L | High (volatile) |
| PPF | 15 years | 7.1% | Tax-free | Very low |
| EPF / VPF | Till retirement | ~8.25% | Mostly tax-free | Very low |
| NSC | 5 years | ~7.7% | Interest taxable | Very low |
| Tax-saver FD | 5 years | ~6.5–7.5% | Interest taxable | Very low |
| SSY | Till girl child is 21 | ~8.2% | Tax-free | Very low |
| Life insurance premium | Policy term | Endowment: ~4–6% | Mostly tax-free | Low, but a poor product (Chapter 3.8) |
| Home loan principal | — | — | — | — |
| Children's tuition fees | — | — | — | — |

### Using ELSS well
- **Old-regime taxpayers:** first count automatic 80C items (EPF, home loan principal, tuition fees, insurance premiums you already pay). Fill only the **remaining gap** with ELSS (if you want equity) or PPF (if you want debt).
- **Don't redeem automatically at 3 years.** The lock-in is a minimum, not a maturity date. If the fund is still good, stay invested.
- **One ELSS fund is enough.**
- **New-regime taxpayers:** ELSS has no tax benefit. A regular flexi-cap or index fund is equivalent without the lock-in.

## 2. Fixed Deposits (FDs)

### How they work
You deposit a lump sum with a bank (or NBFC) for a **fixed term** (7 days to 10 years) at a **fixed interest rate**.

### Types
| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Cumulative** | Interest compounds (usually quarterly) and is paid at maturity. Best for growth. |
| **Non-cumulative** | Interest paid out monthly, quarterly or annually. For income needs. |
| **Tax-saver FD** | 5-year lock-in, 80C deduction (old regime), no premature withdrawal |
| **Sweep-in / auto-sweep FD** | Linked to your savings account. Excess balance is auto-converted into an FD and broken automatically when needed. Good for the emergency fund. |
| **Senior citizen FD** | Usually +0.25–0.50% extra interest for those aged 60+ |
| **Corporate / NBFC FD** | Higher rates (8–9%), **but not DICGC-insured**. Check the credit rating (prefer AAA). |
| **Small Finance Bank FDs** | Often 0.5–1.5% higher than big banks, and DICGC-insured up to ₹5 L |

### Compounding and effective yield
Banks usually compound **quarterly**. A 7% FD compounded quarterly gives an effective annual yield of (1 + 0.07/4)^4 − 1 = **7.19%**.

### Safety: DICGC insurance
- The **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation** (an RBI subsidiary) insures deposits up to **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** (principal + interest combined), across all accounts (savings, FD, RD) **in the same bank**.
- It covers all commercial banks (public, private, foreign), small finance banks, payments banks, regional rural banks and co-operative banks.
- **Not covered:** company or NBFC FDs.
- **Practical rule:** with large sums, spread FDs across banks so each stays under ₹5 L, or use large, systemically important banks.

### Premature withdrawal
Usually allowed (except tax-saver FDs) with a **penalty of 0.5–1%** on the rate applicable for the period the deposit was actually held.

### Taxation
- **Interest is taxed at your slab rate**, on an **accrual basis**, every year, even for cumulative FDs where you receive nothing until maturity.
- **TDS:** banks deduct **10% TDS** if interest from that bank exceeds the threshold (₹50,000/year for non-seniors and ₹1 lakh for seniors, as revised from FY 2025-26). The TDS is just an advance tax. You must still pay the difference if you're in a higher bracket.
- **Form 15G/15H:** if your total income is below the taxable limit, submit this to avoid TDS.

### FD laddering
Instead of one large FD, split money into FDs maturing at **different times** (e.g. 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 years). Each year, one FD matures, giving you liquidity, and you reinvest it at the 5-year rate. This reduces reinvestment risk and keeps money accessible.

### When FDs make sense
- Emergency fund (sweep FDs)
- Short-term goals (< 3 years) where you want certainty
- Low-tax-bracket investors (post-tax returns are better)
- Senior citizens wanting predictable income
- **Not** for long-term wealth: post-tax real returns are near zero or negative (Chapter 1.2)

## 3. Post office small savings schemes

Run by the government through post offices and some banks. All carry a **sovereign guarantee**. Rates are **reset every quarter**.

| Scheme | Who | Tenure | Rate (indicative) | Tax | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PPF** | Any resident | 15 yrs | 7.1% | EEE | Chapter 3.3 |
| **NSC (National Savings Certificate)** | Any resident | 5 yrs | ~7.7% | 80C on deposit (old); **interest taxable** (but reinvested interest of years 1–4 counts toward 80C) | Lump sum. No premature withdrawal except in special cases. |
| **SCSS (Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme)** | Aged 60+ (55+ for some retirees) | 5 yrs (extendable 3 yrs) | ~8.2% | 80C on deposit (old); interest taxable | Max ₹30 L. Quarterly interest. Excellent for retirees. |
| **SSY (Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana)** | Girl child under 10 (parent opens) | Till 21 yrs (deposits for 15 yrs) | ~8.2% | **EEE** | Max ₹1.5 L/yr. Partial withdrawal at 18 for education/marriage. |
| **KVP (Kisan Vikas Patra)** | Any resident | Doubles money in ~115 months | ~7.5% | Interest taxable, no 80C | Simple doubling instrument |
| **POMIS (Monthly Income Scheme)** | Any resident | 5 yrs | ~7.4% | Interest taxable | Monthly income. Max ₹9 L single / ₹15 L joint. |
| **Post Office Time Deposit** | Any resident | 1/2/3/5 yrs | ~6.9–7.5% | Taxable (5-yr qualifies for 80C) | Like a bank FD |
| **Post Office RD** | Any resident | 5 yrs | ~6.7% | Taxable | Monthly deposits |

**When useful:** SSY for parents of a girl child (tax-free, high rate), SCSS for retirees, PPF for everyone. NSC, KVP and POMIS are mainly for very conservative investors.

## 4. RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds
- Issued by the Government of India through RBI and banks.
- **Interest rate = prevailing NSC rate + 0.35%**, reset every 6 months (so ~8.05% when NSC is 7.7%).
- **7-year lock-in** (earlier exit allowed for seniors).
- Interest is paid **half-yearly** and is **fully taxable**.
- Sovereign safety, but taxable and illiquid. Suits low-bracket, income-seeking investors (such as retirees).

## 5. Gold: all the ways to own it

### 5.1 Physical gold
| Form | Problems |
|---|---|
| **Jewellery** | **Making charges of 8–25%**, plus 3% GST on the gold and making charges. On resale, jewellers deduct making charges and impurity. Effectively a large instant loss. |
| **Coins / bars** (banks, jewellers) | Lower making charges (2–8%), but storage and theft risk. Banks often **won't buy back** the coins they sold. Purity concerns (check for the **BIS hallmark / HUID**). |

Fine for cultural and personal use. **Poor as an investment.**

### 5.2 Gold ETFs
- Exchange-traded funds, each unit backed by **physical 99.5% pure gold** held in vaults by a custodian.
- **Bought on NSE/BSE** through a demat account. 1 unit ≈ the price of ~1 gram or less, depending on the ETF.
- **Expense ratio:** ~0.3–0.8%.
- **Pros:** pure, liquid, transparent pricing, no storage or making charges.
- **Cons:** needs demat, brokerage, bid–ask spread (pick ETFs with good volume).

### 5.3 Gold mutual funds (Gold FoFs)
- Mutual funds that invest in the AMC's own gold ETF.
- **No demat needed**, SIPs are easy, buy at NAV.
- **Cost:** the FoF's expense ratio plus the underlying ETF's (total ~0.5–1%).

### 5.4 Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)
- Issued by **RBI on behalf of the Government of India**, priced in grams of gold.
- **2.5% annual interest** on the initial investment value, paid half-yearly (taxable at slab).
- **Tenure: 8 years** (early redemption allowed from year 5 on interest dates).
- **Capital gains on redemption at maturity are tax-free.** This is the big advantage.
- **Current status:** the government **stopped issuing new SGB tranches** (the last issue was in early 2024), citing high costs as gold prices surged. Existing SGBs **trade on NSE/BSE** in a demat account, often at a premium or discount to the gold price, with thin trading volumes.
- If you buy SGBs on the exchange and hold them to maturity, the tax-free benefit still applies.

### 5.5 Digital gold
- Sold by apps and wallets (backed by companies like MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold, Augmont).
- **Not regulated by SEBI or RBI.** SEBI has barred brokers from selling it.
- Spreads of 3% GST + 2–3% buy–sell difference.
- **Avoid.** Use gold ETFs or funds instead.

### 5.6 Comparison
| | Physical | Gold ETF | Gold FoF | SGB (secondary market) | Digital gold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Purity | Varies | 99.5% | Via ETF | Gold price linked | Claimed 99.9% |
| Extra return | None | None | None | **+2.5%/yr interest** | None |
| Costs | High (making, GST) | ~0.3–0.8%/yr | ~0.5–1%/yr | None (spread on purchase) | 3% GST + spreads |
| Regulation | — | SEBI | SEBI | RBI/GoI | **None** |
| Demat needed | No | Yes | No | Yes | No |
| Tax | LTCG 12.5% after 24 months | LTCG 12.5% after 12 months | LTCG 12.5% after 24 months | **Tax-free at maturity**; else LTCG 12.5% after 12 months | As physical |
| Verdict | Avoid for investment | **Good** | **Good** (simple SIPs) | Good if available at a fair price | Avoid |

(Tax rules: Chapter 3.5. Gold FoF holding period and treatment have been revised in recent budgets, so verify.)

### 5.7 How much gold?
- **5–15% of your portfolio**, as a diversifier and crisis hedge.
- Gold can go nowhere for years (2012–2018) and then rally hard (2019–2020, 2024–25). Don't chase it after a big run.
- Silver ETFs and FoFs also exist, but silver is far more volatile (it's also an industrial metal). Optional and small, if at all.

## 6. Master table: when each instrument makes sense

| Need | Best options | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergency fund** | Savings + sweep FD + liquid / overnight fund | Equity, PPF, long FDs, gold |
| **Goal < 1 year** | Liquid fund, short FD, arbitrage fund (30% bracket) | Equity |
| **Goal 1–3 years** | FD ladder, short-duration / corporate bond / banking-PSU fund, target maturity fund | Equity, credit risk funds |
| **Goal 3–5 years** | Debt funds + 20–40% equity (or conservative/balanced hybrid) | 100% equity |
| **Long-term growth (7+ yrs)** | Equity index funds (+ flexi cap), NPS equity | FDs alone, endowment plans |
| **Long-term safe debt** | EPF/VPF, PPF, gilt / target maturity funds | Credit risk funds, company FDs |
| **80C (old regime)** | EPF (automatic), PPF, ELSS, SSY (if eligible) | Endowment / money-back / ULIPs |
| **Extra retirement + tax** | NPS (80CCD(1B) old; 80CCD(2) both) | Insurance "pension plans" |
| **Retiree income** | SCSS, RBI Floating Rate Bonds, POMIS, debt-fund SWP, annuity (part) | Equity-heavy income plans |
| **Girl child's future** | SSY + equity index fund | Child insurance plans |
| **Diversifier / hedge** | Gold ETF or gold FoF (SGB on the secondary market) | Jewellery, digital gold |

## 7. Summary
- **ELSS:** the shortest 80C lock-in (3 years) with equity growth. Old regime only. Fill the remaining 80C gap after automatic items.
- **FDs:** safe (DICGC up to ₹5 L per bank) and predictable, but taxed at slab every year. Use them for short-term goals, emergencies (sweep) and laddering.
- **Small savings schemes:** sovereign-backed. **SSY** (girl child) and **SCSS** (retirees) are standouts.
- **Gold:** use **ETFs or gold FoFs**. SGBs are tax-free at maturity but are only available second-hand now. Avoid digital gold and investment jewellery. Keep it to 5–15%.
