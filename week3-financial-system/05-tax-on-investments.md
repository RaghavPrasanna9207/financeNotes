# Chapter 3.5: How Investments Are Taxed (Capital Gains and More)

> **Core**: §1–3 only. The rest is **Reference: read when you file taxes**

> **What you'll learn:** what a capital gain is, short-term vs long-term, the current tax rates for equity, debt funds, gold, real estate and others, the ₹1.25 L LTCG exemption and tax-gain harvesting, how SIP units are taxed (FIFO), grandfathering, set-off and carry-forward of losses, tax on interest and dividends, TDS, and the Income-tax Act 2025 transition.

> **Important:** figures as of October 2026. These rules reflect the changes in **Budget July 2024** (effective 23 July 2024) and later budgets (including Budget 2026's SGB change), carried into the **Income-tax Act, 2025** (which replaced the 1961 Act from **1 April 2026**). The new Act renumbers sections, but rates and concepts are largely unchanged. A **4% health & education cess** applies on top of all tax, plus a surcharge for high incomes. Always verify current rules on incometax.gov.in or with a tax professional before filing.

## 1. Capital gains basics

### What is a capital gain?
```
Capital gain = Sale price (redemption value) − Purchase cost (cost of acquisition)
```
- **Taxed only when you sell (redeem, switch or transfer).** Holding an investment that has risen in value is **not** taxed.
- **Switching** between funds, even within the same AMC (e.g. regular → direct), counts as a **sale** for tax purposes.
- Gains are taxed in the **financial year** (April–March) of sale.

### Short-term vs long-term
The tax rate depends on **how long you held** the asset before selling:
- **Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG):** held for less than the threshold period
- **Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG):** held for longer than the threshold

**Thresholds (post-July 2024):**
- **Listed securities** (listed shares, equity funds, listed ETFs, listed bonds, REITs/InvITs): **12 months**
- **Everything else** (unlisted shares, real estate, physical gold, unlisted bonds, and most FoFs): **24 months**

## 2. Equity: shares and equity-oriented funds

**Equity-oriented funds** = mutual funds with **≥65% in Indian listed equity** (includes ELSS, aggressive hybrid, arbitrage and equity savings funds, and index funds and ETFs on Indian equity indices).

| | Holding period | Tax rate |
|---|---|---|
| **STCG** | ≤ 12 months | **20%** (flat, regardless of your slab) |
| **LTCG** | > 12 months | **12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year** |

- These rates apply only where **STT (Securities Transaction Tax)** was paid, which is automatically the case for exchange trades and equity fund redemptions.
- **No indexation** benefit.

### The ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption
- The first **₹1.25 L of total equity LTCG per person per financial year** is tax-free. Only gains above that are taxed at 12.5%.
- **Example:** in a financial year you sell equity funds and realise ₹3,00,000 of LTCG.
  - Taxable LTCG = 3,00,000 − 1,25,000 = 1,75,000
  - Tax = 12.5% × 1,75,000 = **₹21,875** + 4% cess = **₹22,750**

### Tax-gain harvesting
The exemption is **use-it-or-lose-it** each year. A simple, legal strategy:
1. Each financial year (say, in February or March), check your unrealised **long-term** equity gains.
2. **Sell** units with gains of up to about ₹1.25 L, and **immediately buy them back** (same or similar fund).
3. You pay **zero tax**, and your **purchase cost resets higher**, which reduces future taxable gains.
4. Over 20 years, this can save lakhs of rupees in tax.

**Cautions:** check exit loads (sell only units held more than 12 months, which are usually past the exit-load period too). It's done per person, so each family member has their own ₹1.25 L. Account for the 1–3 day gap when you're out of the market.

### Grandfathering (for old investments)
LTCG on equity was **tax-free until 31 January 2018**. For shares or equity funds bought before that date, the cost of acquisition is taken as the **higher of** (a) the actual cost and (b) the market value on **31 January 2018** (capped at the sale price). Gains up to that date stay effectively untaxed. This only matters if you hold very old investments.

## 3. How SIP redemptions are taxed: FIFO

Each SIP instalment is a **separate purchase** with its own date and cost. When you redeem, units are sold on a **First-In, First-Out (FIFO)** basis: the oldest units go first.

### Example
You've done a ₹10,000 monthly SIP for 18 months. You redeem everything today.
- Instalments 1–6 (held more than 12 months) → **LTCG**
- Instalments 7–18 (held 12 months or less) → **STCG at 20%**

**Tip:** if you need to redeem only part of your holding, redeem an amount that covers only the oldest (long-term) units. Your platform or CAMS statement shows the units' ages, and tools like the "capital gains statement" from CAMS/KFintech help.

## 4. Debt mutual funds

### Units bought on or after 1 April 2023 (the "specified mutual fund" rule)
For funds investing **≤35% in Indian equity** (most debt funds, gold funds, international FoFs and some hybrids at the time of the change):
- **All gains are taxed at your income-tax slab rate**, regardless of holding period.
- **No indexation**, no LTCG benefit.

From **FY 2025-26**, the "specified mutual fund" definition was narrowed to funds investing **more than 65% in debt and money-market instruments**. That means funds like **gold funds, international FoFs and some hybrids** are no longer covered by the slab-rate rule and get normal capital-gains treatment (12.5% LTCG after 24 months for unlisted units, or after 12 months for listed ETFs). Verify the current position for any specific fund.

### Units bought before 1 April 2023 (grandfathered)
- Held **> 24 months** (post-July 2024): LTCG at **12.5%** without indexation
- Held **≤ 24 months**: STCG at slab

### Debt fund vs FD after tax
Both are taxed at slab rate now. Differences:
| | FD | Debt fund |
|---|---|---|
| When taxed | **Every year** (accrual), even if not withdrawn | **Only on redemption** (deferral) |
| TDS | Yes, above the threshold | No TDS for residents |
| Set-off of losses | N/A | Capital losses can be set off |
| Returns | Fixed | Market-linked (can be higher or lower) |

**Tax deferral matters:** with a debt fund, the money that would have gone to tax every year stays invested and compounds until you redeem.

## 5. Hybrid funds
Tax depends on the fund's **equity percentage**:
| Fund's Indian equity share | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **≥ 65%** (aggressive hybrid, arbitrage, equity savings, most BAFs) | **Equity**: STCG 20% / LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 L (12-month threshold) |
| **35–65%** (some balanced or multi-asset funds) | **Non-equity, non-specified**: LTCG 12.5% after 24 months; STCG at slab |
| **≤ 35%** with > 65% debt (conservative hybrid) | **Debt**: slab rate (if bought after 1 April 2023) |

Check the fund's tax classification on the AMC website.

## 6. Gold

| Gold form | Long-term after | LTCG | STCG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold ETFs (listed) | **12 months** | **12.5%** | Slab |
| Gold mutual funds (FoF) | 24 months (treatment revised from FY 2025-26; verify) | 12.5% | Slab |
| Physical gold / jewellery / digital gold | **24 months** | 12.5% (no indexation) | Slab |
| **SGB held to maturity by an original subscriber** (bought at the RBI issue, held continuously) | n/a | **Exempt** | n/a |
| SGB bought on NSE/BSE, held to maturity (from 1 April 2026) | 12 months | 12.5% | Slab |
| SGB sold on the exchange before maturity | 12 months | 12.5% | Slab |
| SGB interest (2.5%) | n/a | Taxed at slab yearly | |

Budget 2026 limited the SGB maturity exemption to original subscribers, from 1 April 2026. No new SGBs have been issued since early 2024, so new investors should use gold ETFs or gold FoFs.

## 7. Real estate
- **Long-term after 24 months.**
- **LTCG at 12.5% without indexation** (post-July 2024). For land or buildings acquired **before 23 July 2024**, resident individuals can choose the **lower of** 12.5% without indexation or 20% with indexation.
- **STCG at slab.**
- **Exemptions** for reinvesting gains: **Section 54** (buy or construct another residential house), **54EC** (invest up to ₹50 L in specified bonds within 6 months, 5-year lock-in), **54F** (for non-house assets reinvested in a house). Old section names. Check the new Act's equivalents.

---

## 8. International funds and foreign stocks
- **International FoFs (Indian funds investing abroad):** see the specified mutual fund note above. Units bought from 1 April 2023 to 31 March 2025 are taxed at slab. Treatment changed from FY 2025-26.
- **US stocks or ETFs bought directly (via LRS):** treated as unlisted in India. LTCG after **24 months** at 12.5%. STCG at slab. Dividends taxed in the US (25% for Indians under the treaty) with credit available in India. Must be disclosed in **Schedule FA** of your ITR.

## 9. Interest and dividends

| Income | Tax |
|---|---|
| Savings account interest | Slab rate. Old regime: deduction up to ₹10,000 under **80TTA** (₹50,000 for seniors under 80TTB). |
| FD / RD interest | Slab rate, yearly (accrual). TDS 10% above the threshold. |
| NSC, SCSS, POMIS, KVP interest | Slab rate |
| PPF, EPF (within limits), SSY interest | **Tax-free** |
| Corporate bond / NCD interest | Slab rate |
| **Dividends** (from shares or IDCW from funds) | **Slab rate.** TDS 10% if dividends from one company or fund exceed ₹10,000 in a year (threshold revised from ₹5,000). |

## 10. Set-off and carry-forward of losses

Capital losses can reduce your taxable capital gains:

| Loss | Can be set off against |
|---|---|
| **Short-term capital loss (STCL)** | **Both** STCG and LTCG |
| **Long-term capital loss (LTCL)** | **Only** LTCG |
| Capital losses | **Cannot** be set off against salary or other income |

- **Unabsorbed capital losses** can be **carried forward for 8 years**, but **only if you file your ITR on time** (by the due date).
- **Tax-loss harvesting:** if you have short-term losses on some holdings, booking them can offset gains elsewhere. (India has no formal "wash sale" rule, but avoid transactions done purely for tax that lack substance. Be reasonable.)

## 11. TDS and reporting
- **Resident investors:** no TDS on mutual fund or share capital gains. You pay tax when you file your return (or as **advance tax** if your total liability exceeds ₹10,000 in the year).
- **NRIs:** TDS is deducted on capital gains at the applicable rates.
- **Capital gains statement:** download from **CAMS / KFintech / MF Central** (mutual funds) and your broker (shares). These give the exact STCG and LTCG for your ITR.
- **AIS (Annual Information Statement)** on the income tax portal shows most interest, dividends and transactions reported about you. Cross-check it before filing.
- With capital gains, you generally need to file **ITR-2** (salaried with capital gains) or **ITR-3** (business income), not ITR-1. ITR-1 and ITR-4 now allow limited equity LTCG within the ₹1.25 L exemption. Check the year's forms.

## 13. Summary
- Tax is due only when you **sell or switch**.
- **Equity:** STCG **20%** (≤ 12 months), LTCG **12.5% above ₹1.25 L/year**. **Harvest the ₹1.25 L every year.**
- **SIP units:** FIFO. Each instalment has its own holding period.
- **Debt funds bought since April 2023:** taxed at **slab** (but deferred until redemption, unlike FDs).
- **Gold ETFs:** 12.5% after 12 months. **SGBs at maturity:** tax-free only for original subscribers who held continuously. Exchange buyers pay 12.5% LTCG (or slab if held 12 months or less).
- **Losses:** STCL offsets both, LTCL only LTCG. Carry forward 8 years if you **file on time**.
- Use **CAMS/KFintech capital gains statements and AIS** when filing.

## Self-check questions
1. You redeem equity fund units bought 10 months ago with a ₹40,000 gain. How much tax (before cess)?
2. In a year you book ₹1,00,000 of equity LTCG. How much tax?
3. Explain tax-gain harvesting in two sentences.
4. You have a ₹50,000 long-term capital loss and a ₹30,000 short-term capital gain. Can you set one off against the other?
5. Why is a debt fund more tax-efficient than an FD even though both are taxed at slab?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. STCG at 20% = ₹8,000.
2. Zero. It's within the ₹1.25 L exemption.
3. Each year, sell long-term equity holdings with gains up to ₹1.25 L and buy them back immediately. You pay no tax and your cost basis rises, which reduces future taxable gains.
4. No. Long-term losses can only be set off against long-term gains. Carry the LTCL forward (up to 8 years, if you file on time).
5. FD interest is taxed every year as it accrues. Debt fund gains are taxed only on redemption, so the deferred tax keeps compounding.
</details>
