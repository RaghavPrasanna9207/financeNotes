# Chapter 3.9: Week 3 Output: Your One-Page Money Plan

> **Goal:** write a single page that says how much you invest, where it goes and why. It pulls together everything from Weeks 1–3. Keep it to **one page**, print it or pin it, and review it once a year.

Allow 60–90 minutes. Fill in your real numbers, even if they're small.

## Worked example: a 22-year-old in their first job

**Snapshot:** take-home ₹60,000/month. Expenses ₹30,000 (rent share, food, transport, phone). Surplus ₹30,000 → **savings rate 50%**. No loans. EPF auto-deducted (₹2,400 + employer share). Savings ₹40,000.

**Safety net:**
- Emergency fund target: 5 × ₹30,000 = **₹1.5 L**. Build it in 4 months: ₹25,000/month to a liquid fund, ₹5,000/month to SIPs meanwhile.
- Health: employer ₹5 L + **own ₹10 L policy** (~₹8–10k/year) + ₹50 L super top-up later.
- Term life: **not needed yet** (no dependents, no loans). Revisit at marriage or the first loan.
- PA cover: ₹25 L (~₹1,500/year).

**Goals:**
| Goal | Today's cost | Years | Future cost | SIP | Mix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency fund | ₹1.5 L | 0.33 | ₹1.5 L | ₹25,000 for 4 months | Liquid fund |
| Higher studies (MBA) | ₹20 L | 5 | ~₹29 L (8% edu. inflation) | ~₹38,000* at 10% (too high → partial loan/scholarship, or adjust) | 40% equity / 60% debt |
| Retirement / financial independence | — | 35+ | — | Everything else | 80/15/5 |

*Seeing that number is useful. It shows the MBA needs a mix of savings and an education loan, or a longer timeline.

**Allocation (long-term money, after the emergency fund is built):**
| Asset | % | Instrument | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equity | 80% | Nifty 50 index fund ₹15,000 + Nifty Next 50 index fund ₹5,000 | ₹20,000 |
| Debt | 15% | EPF (auto, counts toward debt) + PPF ₹2,500 | ₹2,500 (+ EPF) |
| Gold | 5% | Gold fund | ₹1,250 |
| Short-term goal (MBA) | — | Short-duration debt fund ₹4,000 + flexi cap ₹2,250 | ₹6,250 |

**Tax:** income about ₹9 L → **new regime, zero tax**. No ELSS needed.

**Why:** "I'm 22 with a 35+ year horizon, a stable job, no dependents and an emergency fund, so I can hold mostly equity. Index funds keep costs low. If markets fall 40%, my SIPs keep running."
