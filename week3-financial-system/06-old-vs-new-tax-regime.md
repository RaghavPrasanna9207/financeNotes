# Chapter 3.6: Income Tax Basics: Old vs New Regime

> **What you'll learn:** how Indian income tax is structured (heads of income, gross vs taxable income, slabs, rebate, cess, surcharge), the slab rates in both regimes, every major deduction in the old regime, what's still allowed in the new regime, worked comparisons at different salaries, how to choose, and practical filing basics.

> Rates reflect **Budget 2025** (applicable from FY 2025-26), carried into the **Income-tax Act, 2025** (from FY 2026-27). Section numbers below are the **old (1961 Act)** names that people still use. The new Act renumbers them. Verify current rates on incometax.gov.in.

---

## 1. How income tax works: the structure

### Step 1: Add up income under the five "heads"
1. **Salaries** (salary, bonus, allowances, perquisites)
2. **Income from house property** (rent)
3. **Profits and gains of business or profession** (freelancing, business)
4. **Capital gains** (Chapter 3.5)
5. **Income from other sources** (interest, dividends, gifts above limits)

Total = **Gross Total Income**.

### Step 2: Subtract deductions and exemptions
→ **Taxable Income** (also called Total Income)

### Step 3: Apply slab rates
Tax is **progressive**: each slice of income is taxed at its own rate. **Moving into a higher slab only taxes the extra income at the higher rate, not your whole income.**

### Step 4: Subtract the rebate (Section 87A), if eligible

### Step 5: Add surcharge (high incomes: above ₹50 L) and **4% health & education cess** on the tax

### Step 6: Subtract TDS and advance tax already paid → **tax payable or refund**

### Financial year vs assessment year
- **Financial Year (FY):** the year you earn income (1 April – 31 March), e.g. FY 2026-27.
- **Assessment Year (AY):** the following year, when it's assessed and the return is filed, e.g. AY 2027-28. (The new Act simplifies this to a single "tax year" concept.)
- **ITR due date** for most salaried individuals: **31 July** after the FY ends (sometimes extended).

## 2. The new tax regime (the default)

Since FY 2023-24, the **new regime is the default**. You must actively opt for the old regime if you want it.

### Slab rates (FY 2025-26 onwards)
| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | **Nil** |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

### Key features
- **Standard deduction:** **₹75,000** for salaried employees and pensioners.
- **Rebate under 87A:** if taxable income is **≤ ₹12,00,000**, the tax is fully rebated (up to ₹60,000), so **zero tax**.
  - For a salaried person: ₹12,00,000 + ₹75,000 standard deduction = **₹12.75 L gross salary, zero tax**.
  - **Marginal relief:** if taxable income is slightly above ₹12 L, the tax payable is capped at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 L. (At ₹12.1 L taxable, the tax is ₹10,000, not the ₹61,500 the slabs would give.)
  - **The rebate doesn't apply to special-rate income** like STCG on equity (20%) or LTCG (12.5%). Those are taxed separately even if your other income is under ₹12 L.
- **Lower rates, but almost no deductions.**

### What IS allowed in the new regime
| Allowed | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Standard deduction ₹75,000** | Salaried and pensioners |
| **Employer's NPS contribution, 80CCD(2)** | Up to **14% of basic + DA** |
| Employer's EPF contribution (within limits) | Not taxable up to 12% of basic |
| **Family pension deduction** | ₹25,000 |
| **Agniveer Corpus Fund** | 80CCH |
| Home loan interest on a **let-out** property | Against rental income (with limits on loss set-off) |
| Gratuity, leave encashment (within limits), VRS compensation | Exempt as usual |
| PPF, EPF, SSY interest | Still tax-free (that's an exemption of the income, not a deduction) |

### What is NOT allowed in the new regime
80C (EPF, PPF, ELSS, LIC premium, tuition, home loan principal…), 80D (health insurance), 80CCD(1B) (own NPS), 80E (education loan interest), 80G (donations), 80TTA/TTB, **HRA exemption**, **LTA**, **home loan interest on a self-occupied house (Section 24(b))**, professional tax, most allowances.

## 3. The old tax regime

### Slab rates (individuals below 60)
| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | Nil |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

- **Seniors (60–80):** nil up to ₹3 L. **Super seniors (80+):** nil up to ₹5 L.
- **Standard deduction:** **₹50,000** (salaried).
- **Rebate 87A:** zero tax if taxable income ≤ **₹5,00,000**.

### Main deductions and exemptions in the old regime

| Section | What | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **80C** | EPF (employee share), VPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums, NSC, 5-yr tax-saver FD, SSY, SCSS, **home loan principal**, **children's tuition fees** (2 kids), stamp duty on a house purchase | **₹1,50,000** combined |
| **80CCD(1)** | Own NPS contribution | Within the ₹1.5 L 80C limit |
| **80CCD(1B)** | Own NPS contribution | **Extra ₹50,000** |
| **80CCD(2)** | Employer NPS | 10% of basic + DA (private employer) |
| **80D** | Health insurance premiums | Self/family: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if senior). Parents: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if senior). Preventive check-up ₹5,000 within these. **Max ₹1,00,000** |
| **80E** | Education loan **interest** | No limit, for 8 years |
| **80G** | Donations to eligible charities | 50% or 100% of the donation, with some limits |
| **80TTA** | Savings account interest | ₹10,000 (non-seniors) |
| **80TTB** | All deposit interest (seniors) | ₹50,000 |
| **24(b)** | **Home loan interest**, self-occupied house | **₹2,00,000** |
| **HRA (Sec 10(13A))** | House rent allowance exemption | Least of: actual HRA; 50% of basic (metro) / 40% (non-metro); rent paid − 10% of basic |
| **LTA** | Leave travel allowance | Actual travel fare, 2 journeys in a 4-year block |
| Professional tax | | Up to ₹2,500 |

### HRA example
Basic salary ₹50,000/month (₹6 L/yr), HRA received ₹25,000/month (₹3 L/yr), rent paid ₹22,000/month (₹2.64 L/yr), living in a metro.
- Actual HRA: ₹3,00,000
- 50% of basic: ₹3,00,000
- Rent − 10% of basic: 2,64,000 − 60,000 = ₹2,04,000
- **Exempt HRA = least = ₹2,04,000**

## 4. Worked comparisons

### Case A: salary ₹10 L, only basic deductions
**New regime:**
- Taxable = 10,00,000 − 75,000 = ₹9,25,000 → ≤ ₹12 L → **Tax = ₹0**

**Old regime** (standard deduction + full 80C):
- Taxable = 10,00,000 − 50,000 − 1,50,000 = ₹8,00,000
- Tax = 12,500 (2.5–5 L) + 60,000 (5–8 L at 20%) = 72,500 + 4% cess = **₹75,400**

→ **New regime wins by ₹75,400.** For anyone with income up to ₹12.75 L, the new regime is almost always better.

### Case B: salary ₹15 L, typical salaried deductions
**New regime:**
- Taxable = 15,00,000 − 75,000 = ₹14,25,000
- Tax = 0 (0–4 L) + 20,000 (4–8 L) + 40,000 (8–12 L) + 33,750 (12–14.25 L at 15%) = 93,750 + cess = **₹97,500**

**Old regime, version 1** (renter): standard ₹50k + 80C ₹1.5 L + 80D ₹25k + NPS 80CCD(1B) ₹50k + HRA exemption ₹2 L = **₹4.75 L**
- Taxable = ₹10,25,000
- Tax = 12,500 + 1,00,000 + 7,500 (above 10 L at 30%) = 1,20,000 + cess = **₹1,24,800**
→ **New regime wins by ₹27,300.**

**Old regime, version 2:** the same person also owns a flat in their home town, bought with a home loan, while renting in the city where they work. HRA and home-loan interest can both be claimed in that situation. Add ₹2 L of home-loan interest (24(b)), so deductions = **₹6.75 L**.
- Taxable = ₹8,25,000
- Tax = 12,500 + 65,000 = 77,500 + cess = **₹80,600**
→ **Old regime wins by ₹16,900.**

### Case C: salary ₹20 L
**New regime:**
- Taxable = ₹19,25,000
- Tax = 20,000 + 40,000 + 60,000 + 65,000 (16–19.25 L at 20%) = 1,85,000 + cess = **₹1,92,400**

**Breakeven:** the old regime wins only if total deductions (including the ₹50k standard deduction) exceed about **₹7.6 L**. That typically requires full 80C + 80D (including parents) + NPS ₹50k + large HRA or home-loan interest.

### Case D: employer NPS in the new regime (salary ₹20 L, basic ₹10 L)
- Employer contributes 14% of basic = ₹1,40,000 to NPS under 80CCD(2) (restructured within the same CTC).
- Taxable = 19,25,000 − 1,40,000 = ₹17,85,000
- Tax = 20,000 + 40,000 + 60,000 + 37,000 = 1,57,000 + cess = **₹1,63,280**
- **Saves ₹29,120 a year** compared with Case C, *and* builds your retirement corpus.

### Approximate breakeven deductions (salaried, illustrative)
| Gross salary | Old regime is better only if total deductions exceed roughly |
|---|---|
| ≤ ₹12.75 L | Practically never |
| ₹15 L | ~₹5.9 L |
| ₹20 L | ~₹7.6 L |
| ₹25 L+ | ~₹8 L+ |

(Exact numbers depend on your situation. Always compute both.)

## 5. How to choose

### The new regime is usually better if:
- Your income is up to **₹12.75 L** (zero tax)
- You don't pay rent (or pay little), have no home loan, and don't max out 80C/80D/NPS
- You value simplicity and **liquidity** (no need to lock money in tax-saving products)

### The old regime may be better if:
- You have a **home loan** on a self-occupied house (₹2 L interest deduction) **and**
- You pay **high rent** in a metro (large HRA exemption) **and/or**
- You max out **80C + 80D (including parents) + 80CCD(1B)**
- Your total deductions exceed the breakeven for your income

### Switching rules
- **Salaried (no business income):** you can choose **every year** when filing your return. You tell your employer your choice at the start of the year (for TDS), but you can still switch when filing.
- **With business or professional income:** you can switch back from the new regime to the old **only once in a lifetime**.

### The golden rule
**Never buy a financial product just to save tax.** Buying an endowment policy or ULIP for 80C can cost you far more in poor returns than the tax saved (Chapter 3.8). Tax saving is a side benefit of good financial decisions, not a reason to make them.

## 6. Practical tax basics for a young earner

### Documents to know
| Document | What it is |
|---|---|
| **PAN** | Permanent Account Number, your tax identity |
| **Form 16** | Issued by your employer: salary paid and TDS deducted |
| **Form 26AS** | Tax credit statement: all TDS and taxes paid against your PAN |
| **AIS / TIS** | Annual Information Statement: interest, dividends, securities transactions, mutual fund transactions and more reported about you |
| **Capital gains statements** | From CAMS/KFintech (mutual funds) and your broker (shares) |

### Filing your ITR
- File on the **income tax e-filing portal** (incometax.gov.in) before the due date (usually **31 July**).
- **ITR-1 (Sahaj):** salaried, income up to ₹50 L, one house property, limited LTCG (≤ ₹1.25 L), no other capital gains.
- **ITR-2:** salaried **with capital gains**, more than one house, foreign assets, etc.
- **ITR-3:** business or professional income (freelancers, F&O traders).
- **ITR-4 (Sugam):** presumptive business income.
- **E-verify** within 30 days (Aadhaar OTP is easiest).

### Why file even if your income is below taxable limits?
- To **claim refunds** of TDS (e.g. on FDs)
- To **carry forward capital losses** (only allowed if filed on time)
- Proof of income for **loans, visas and credit cards**
- Required if you have foreign assets, high-value transactions, etc.

### Advance tax
If your total tax liability for the year (after TDS) exceeds **₹10,000**, you must pay **advance tax** in instalments: 15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December and 100% by 15 March. This is relevant if you have large capital gains, rental or freelance income. Otherwise interest is charged (Sections 234B/234C).

## 7. Summary

- Tax is **progressive**. Higher slabs apply only to the extra income.
- **New regime (default):** slabs 0/5/10/15/20/25/30% with breakpoints every ₹4 L, a **₹75k standard deduction**, and **zero tax up to ₹12 L taxable (₹12.75 L salary)**, with marginal relief. Very few deductions, but **employer NPS (80CCD(2)) is still allowed**.
- **Old regime:** slabs 0/5/20/30%, a ₹50k standard deduction, and many deductions (80C ₹1.5 L, 80D, 80CCD(1B) ₹50k, HRA, home loan interest ₹2 L…).
- Old wins only with **large deductions**: roughly ₹6 L+ at ₹15 L salary, ₹7.6 L+ at ₹20 L.
- Salaried people can switch every year. **Compute both every year.**
- **Never buy bad products to save tax.**
