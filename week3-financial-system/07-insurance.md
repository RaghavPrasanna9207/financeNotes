## 1. What insurance is for

### The principle
Insurance **transfers a risk you can't afford to bear yourself** to an insurance company, in exchange for a small, certain payment (the **premium**). The insurer pools premiums from many people and pays the few who suffer a loss.

**Insure against events that would be financially devastating, not small, manageable expenses.**
| Insure | Don't bother insuring |
|---|---|
| Death of the main earner (family loses income) | Phone screen cracks |
| Major hospitalisation (₹5–30 L) | Small doctor visits |
| Permanent disability | Extended warranties on gadgets |
| Liability, home damage from disasters | |

### Insurance is not an investment
The most important lesson of the module: **insurance and investment are two separate jobs.** Products that combine them (endowment, money-back, ULIPs) do both jobs badly (Chapter 3.8). A pure insurance product that pays nothing if nothing bad happens is **working exactly as it should**.

### Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Premium** | What you pay (yearly or monthly) |
| **Sum insured / sum assured** | The maximum the insurer pays (sum insured for health, sum assured for life) |
| **Policy term** | How long the cover lasts |
| **Proposer / insured / nominee** | Who buys / whose life or health is covered / who receives the claim |
| **Claim settlement ratio (CSR)** | % of claims (by number) the insurer settled in a year |
| **Exclusions** | What the policy won't cover |
| **Waiting period** | Time before certain conditions are covered |
| **Rider** | An add-on cover attached to a base policy |
| **Free look period** | 15–30 days after purchase to cancel for a refund if you're unhappy |

### The regulator: IRDAI
The **Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India** regulates insurers, products and claims. Complaints go first to the insurer's grievance cell, then to the **Bima Bharosa portal** and the **Insurance Ombudsman** (free).

## 2. Health insurance

### Why everyone needs it
- A single hospitalisation for something serious (cardiac surgery, cancer treatment, a severe accident, ICU care) can cost **₹5–30 lakh** at a good private hospital.
- **Medical inflation in India runs at about 10–14% a year**, much faster than general inflation.
- Without insurance, one event can wipe out years of savings and investments.
- **Buy young:** premiums are lowest, there are no pre-existing conditions, and waiting periods finish while you're healthy.

### Types of health policies
| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Individual** | Cover for one person |
| **Family floater** | One sum insured shared by the family (e.g. ₹10 L for self + spouse + kids). Cheaper, but one big claim uses up the cover for everyone. |
| **Senior citizen plans** | For parents. Expensive, with co-pays and limits. Better than nothing. |
| **Group / employer health insurance** | Provided by your employer |
| **Top-up** | Pays above a **deductible** per claim |
| **Super top-up** | Pays above a deductible on the **total of all claims in the year**. Much better than a plain top-up. |
| **Critical illness** | A lump sum on diagnosis (section 5) |

### How much cover?
| Situation | Suggested base cover |
|---|---|
| Single, young, tier-2 city | ₹5–10 L |
| Single, metro | ₹10 L+ |
| Family (floater), metro | ₹15–25 L |
| Parents (separate policy) | ₹10 L+ (often with a super top-up) |

**The smart structure: base policy + super top-up.**
- Base ₹10 L + super top-up of ₹50 L with a ₹10 L deductible = **₹60 L of effective cover**, usually for not much more than a ₹10 L base policy alone. The super top-up is cheap because it pays only for rare, large claims.

### Employer health insurance: why it's not enough
1. **It ends when you leave the job**, or are laid off, which is exactly when you can least afford a gap.
2. **The cover is often small** (₹3–5 L), shared with family.
3. **Buying later is harder:** if you develop a condition (diabetes, BP) while relying on employer cover, a new personal policy will have **waiting periods or exclusions** for it, or may be refused.
4. **Recommendation:** keep a **personal policy** from your 20s, even if small, and treat employer cover as a bonus.

### Features to check (the most important part)
| Feature | What it means | What you want |
|---|---|---|
| **Room rent limit** | A cap on the hospital room category/rent (e.g. 1% of sum insured per day) | **No cap**, or "single private room". **Critical:** if you choose a costlier room than allowed, the insurer **reduces the entire claim proportionately** (doctor fees, tests, everything), not just the room rent. |
| **Co-payment** | You pay a % of every claim (e.g. 10–20%) | **None** (often unavoidable for seniors) |
| **Pre-existing disease (PED) waiting period** | Conditions you have when you buy are covered only after this period | **As short as possible**, ideally 1–3 years. (IRDAI has capped it at 3 years.) |
| **Initial waiting period** | Usually 30 days for illnesses (accidents covered from day 1) | Standard |
| **Specific disease waiting period** | 1–2 years for listed conditions (hernia, cataract, joint replacement…) | Shorter is better |
| **Disease-wise sub-limits** | Caps on specific treatments (e.g. cataract max ₹40,000) | **No sub-limits** |
| **Restoration / recharge** | If you use up your sum insured, it's restored for future claims in the same year | Yes, ideally unlimited, and for the same illness |
| **No-claim bonus (NCB)** | Sum insured increases (e.g. +10–50% a year) for claim-free years | Yes. Some plans offer up to 100%+. |
| **Pre- and post-hospitalisation** | Costs before (30–60 days) and after (60–180 days) the hospital stay | Longer is better |
| **Day-care procedures** | Treatments under 24 hours (chemotherapy, dialysis, cataract) | Covered |
| **Domiciliary / AYUSH / modern treatments** | Home treatment, Ayurveda etc., robotic surgery, immunotherapy | Covered, without tight sub-limits |
| **Network hospitals** | Hospitals with **cashless** tie-ups | A large network including good hospitals **near you** |
| **Claim settlement record** | Ratio and speed of settlement, complaints per 10,000 claims | Higher CSR, lower complaints |
| **Lifelong renewability** | Guaranteed renewal | Yes (now standard by regulation) |

### Cashless vs reimbursement claims
- **Cashless:** at a **network hospital**, the hospital's TPA desk or insurer approves the treatment and settles the bill directly. You pay only non-covered items.
  - Planned treatment: get pre-authorisation in advance. Emergency: inform within 24 hours.
- **Reimbursement:** at a non-network hospital, you pay first, then submit bills, reports and the discharge summary for reimbursement.
- **Keep all documents.** Inform the insurer quickly.

### Portability
You can **port** your health policy to another insurer at renewal **without losing accumulated waiting-period credits** (they carry over for the existing sum insured).

### Disclosure
**Always disclose everything honestly** in the proposal form: existing conditions, medications, smoking and alcohol habits, past surgeries. **Non-disclosure is the #1 reason claims are rejected.** Under the "moratorium" rule, after **5 years** of continuous coverage, an insurer can't reject a claim for non-disclosure except in cases of proven fraud. Still, disclose upfront.

### Tax and GST
- Old regime: **80D deduction** (₹25k self/family + ₹25k–50k parents).
- **GST:** individual health and life insurance premiums have been **exempt from GST since 22 September 2025** (they were previously at 18%).

## 3. Term life insurance

### What it is
**Pure life cover for a fixed term.** If you die during the term, your nominee receives the **sum assured**. If you survive the term, **you get nothing back**. That's why it's so cheap.

### Who needs it
**Anyone whose income others depend on:**
- A spouse who doesn't earn (or earns less)
- Children
- Parents who depend on you
- Anyone who'd inherit large **loans** you've taken (a home loan)

**Who doesn't need it (yet):** students and young earners with **no dependents** and no loans. There's nobody to protect.
- One nuance: buying a modest term policy early (e.g. at 25) locks in a **low premium for life** and avoids future health issues affecting eligibility. It's reasonable if you expect dependents soon.

### How much cover?
**Method 1: Income multiple (quick rule)**
- **10–15× your annual income**, plus outstanding loans. Young people with long careers ahead may go to 20×.

**Method 2: Human Life Value / needs-based (better)**
Add up what your family would need if you weren't there:
1. **Replace income:** annual household expenses (your share) × years until dependents are self-sufficient (adjusted for inflation and investment returns)
2. **Pay off all loans:** home, car, personal
3. **Fund future goals:** children's education, marriage, spouse's retirement
4. **Subtract** existing assets: investments, existing life cover, EPF

**Example:** 30-year-old, ₹12 L annual income.
- Family expenses ₹7 L/yr for ~25 years → corpus needed ≈ ₹1.3–1.6 Cr (depending on inflation and return assumptions)
- Home loan outstanding: ₹40 L
- Children's education: ₹40 L (future value)
- Minus existing investments: −₹15 L
- **Cover needed ≈ ₹2 Cr**

### Policy term
Cover until your **dependents become financially independent** and your **loans are repaid**, typically **until age 60–65**. Cover until 85 or 99 is unnecessary and much more expensive. By then you should have enough wealth to be "self-insured".

### Cost
- A **₹1 crore** cover until age 60 for a **healthy 25–30-year-old non-smoker** typically costs **₹9,000–15,000 a year**.
- **Smokers pay 50–100% more.** Women usually pay slightly less.
- **Premiums are fixed for the entire term** when you buy, so buying young locks in a low price.

### Features and riders
| Feature / rider | Worth it? |
|---|---|
| **Accidental death benefit** | Optional (pays extra on accidental death). A separate personal accident policy is often better. |
| **Critical illness rider** | Optional. A standalone policy may be better. |
| **Waiver of premium** | Useful: future premiums are waived on disability or critical illness |
| **Terminal illness benefit** | Usually built in |
| **Return of Premium (TROP)** | **Avoid.** Much higher premium (often 2× or more), and the "returned" premiums have a poor implicit return (~4–5%). Buy plain term and invest the difference. |
| **Increasing cover** | Niche |
| **Limited pay** (pay for 10–15 years, cover for 30) | Total cost is often higher. Regular pay is generally fine. |
| **Monthly payout to nominee** instead of a lump sum | Option for families who might mismanage a lump sum |

### How to choose an insurer
- **Claim settlement ratio (CSR)** by number **and** by amount: look for 97–99%+ consistently over 3–5 years (from IRDAI's annual report).
- **Size and track record** of the insurer.
- **Claim process and service reviews.**
- Buy **online, directly** from the insurer's website or through reputable aggregators. Premiums are cheaper online.
- **Consider splitting** a large cover across two insurers (e.g. ₹1 Cr + ₹1 Cr) for redundancy.

### Buying process
1. Get quotes, choose the insurer and cover.
2. **Fill in the proposal form honestly:** health history, smoking, alcohol, occupation, income, **existing policies**.
3. **Medical tests** (often at home) and **income proof**.
4. Underwriting → policy issued.
5. **Add your nominee**, and tell your family about the policy (where the documents are, which insurer). Many claims go unclaimed because families don't know a policy exists.
6. Consider the **MWPA (Married Women's Property Act)** option: it puts the payout beyond the reach of creditors, so it goes only to your spouse and children.

### Claims
- The nominee submits a claim form, the death certificate, policy documents, ID proof and bank details.
- **IRDAI rules require settlement within 30 days** for claims not needing investigation.
- Claims within the first **3 years** may be investigated more closely (Section 45 of the Insurance Act). After 3 years, a policy can't be challenged on grounds other than fraud.

## 4. Personal accident (PA) insurance
- Covers **accidental death** and, importantly, **permanent total or partial disability** and sometimes **temporary disability** (weekly income).
- Disability can destroy earning capacity without death, and **term insurance doesn't pay for disability**.
- Cheap: about ₹1,000–3,000 a year for ₹25–50 L cover.
- Also check: **PMSBY** (Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana), a government accident cover of ₹2 L for a ₹20/year premium through banks.

## 5. Critical illness (CI) insurance
- Pays a **lump sum on diagnosis** of a listed serious illness (cancer, heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, major organ transplant…), regardless of actual hospital bills.
- Useful because serious illnesses cause **income loss and non-medical costs** that health insurance doesn't cover.
- Check the list of illnesses, the definitions, the survival period (e.g. must survive 30 days after diagnosis) and the waiting period.
- **Priority:** after health and term insurance. Nice to have if affordable.

## 6. Other covers worth knowing
| Cover | When useful |
|---|---|
| **Home insurance** (structure + contents) | Cheap protection against fire, flood, earthquake, theft |
| **Motor insurance** | Third-party cover is **mandatory**. Comprehensive / own-damage cover is advisable. |
| **Travel insurance** | International trips, often mandatory for visas (Schengen) |
| **PMJJBY** | Government term cover of ₹2 L for about ₹436/year (ages 18–50). A small supplement only. |

## 7. Priority order and a sample setup

**Priority:** 1. Health insurance (everyone) → 2. Term life (if dependents) → 3. Personal accident / disability → 4. Critical illness → 5. Others.

### Sample setups
| Person | Insurance |
|---|---|
| 21-year-old student, no dependents | Covered under parents' family floater, or own ₹5 L policy. No life cover. |
| 25-year-old, first job, no dependents | Own ₹10 L health policy + employer cover. PA cover. Optional: a small term plan to lock in a low rate. |
| 30-year-old, married, one child, home loan | Family floater ₹15–20 L + super top-up ₹50 L. Separate parents' policy. **Term ₹1.5–2 Cr until 60.** PA ₹50 L. |

## 8. Summary
- Insurance **transfers catastrophic risks**. It's an expense, **not an investment**.
- **Health insurance for everyone:** ₹10 L+ base in metros, plus a **super top-up**. **Don't rely only on employer cover.** Check **room rent limits, co-pay, PED waiting period, sub-limits, restoration and network hospitals**.
- **Term life only if someone depends on your income:** 10–15× income (or needs-based), cover until 60–65, buy online, **disclose everything**, tell your family. Avoid return-of-premium plans.
- Add **personal accident/disability** cover cheaply. Critical illness if affordable.
- Individual health and life premiums are **GST-free since September 2025**.

4. A 22-year-old student with no dependents asks if they need term insurance. What do you say?
5. Why is a "Return of Premium" term plan a poor choice?

1. It ends when you leave the job, the cover is often small, and if you develop conditions meanwhile, new personal policies will have waiting periods or exclusions.

3. It covers total annual claims above a deductible. It's cheap because large claims are rare, and paired with a base policy it gives large effective cover.
