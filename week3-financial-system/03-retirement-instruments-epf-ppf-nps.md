# Chapter 3.3: Retirement Instruments: EPF, VPF, PPF and NPS

> **Reference: read when you start earning**

> *Includes material from Varsity's NPS module.*

## Part A: EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)

## 1. What EPF is
A mandatory **retirement savings scheme for salaried employees**, run by the **EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation)** under the EPF & MP Act, 1952.
- Applies to establishments with **20 or more employees**.
- Mandatory for employees earning basic + DA up to **₹15,000/month**. Employees earning more can be covered voluntarily, and most large employers cover everyone.

## 2. How contributions work
Both you and your employer contribute **12% of (basic salary + dearness allowance)** every month.

| Contribution | Goes to |
|---|---|
| **Your 12%** | Entirely to your **EPF** account |
| **Employer's 12%** | **8.33%** to **EPS** (Employees' Pension Scheme), capped at 8.33% of ₹15,000 = **₹1,250/month**. The **remainder (3.67% + any excess above the cap)** goes to your EPF. |

**Example:** basic + DA = ₹40,000/month
- Your contribution: 12% × 40,000 = ₹4,800 → EPF
- Employer: 12% × 40,000 = ₹4,800 → ₹1,250 to EPS, ₹3,550 to EPF
- **Total into EPF each month: ₹8,350.** Into EPS: ₹1,250.

Note: in many CTC-based salary structures, the "employer contribution" is **part of your CTC**. You're effectively paying both halves.

## 3. Interest rate
- Declared **every year** by the EPFO's Central Board of Trustees and approved by the Finance Ministry.
- Recent years: **8.25%** (FY 2023-24, FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26).
- Interest is calculated monthly on the running balance and **credited once a year**.
- Effectively **government-backed** and very safe.

## 4. Tax treatment: EEE (mostly)
- **Contribution:** your contribution qualifies for **80C** (old regime only, within ₹1.5 L).
- **Interest:** tax-free, **except** interest earned on **your own contributions above ₹2.5 L a year** (₹5 L if your employer doesn't contribute). That excess portion's interest is taxable every year.
- **Employer contributions:** taxable as perquisites if the employer's total contributions to **EPF + NPS + superannuation exceed ₹7.5 L a year**.
- **Withdrawal:** tax-free after **5 years of continuous service** (including service with previous employers, if you transferred your EPF). If withdrawn earlier, it's taxable, with TDS.

## 5. Withdrawals
The EPFO reforms of October 2025 changed the withdrawal rules:

- **Full withdrawal:** at retirement (age 58).
- **On losing your job:**
  - up to **75% of the EPF balance** can be withdrawn **immediately**
  - the remaining **25%** only after **12 months of unemployment**
  - the **EPS (pension) amount** only after **36 months of unemployment** (it used to be 2 months)
- **Partial withdrawals:** the old 13 purposes were merged into **3 categories**:
  1. **Essential needs:** illness, education (up to **10 withdrawals**) and marriage (up to **5 withdrawals**)
  2. **Housing:** buying or building a house, or repaying a home loan. Needs **12 months of membership**.
  3. **Special circumstances**
- Claims are filed **online** through the EPFO member portal using your **UAN**.

## 6. Job changes: transfer, don't withdraw
- Your **UAN (Universal Account Number)** stays the same for life. Each employer creates a new **member ID** under it.
- When you change jobs, **transfer the old balance** to the new employer's account (online). Transfers are increasingly automatic.
- **Don't withdraw EPF on job change.** You lose compounding and may pay tax if service is under 5 years.

## 7. EPS (Employees' Pension Scheme)
- The employer's 8.33% (capped) goes here. You don't earn interest on it.
- After **10+ years** of service, you're eligible for a **monthly pension** from age 58, based on a formula: (pensionable salary × service years) / 70. With the ₹15,000 wage cap, the pension is modest.
- With less than 10 years of service, you can withdraw the EPS amount (based on a table) or carry it forward with a scheme certificate. Since October 2025, withdrawing EPS after a job loss needs **36 months of unemployment**.

## 8. VPF (Voluntary Provident Fund)
- You can contribute **more than 12%** (up to 100% of basic + DA) into EPF voluntarily.
- It earns the **same EPF interest rate** (8.25%) with the same safety.
- The employer doesn't match VPF.
- **Tax:** within the ₹2.5 L/year limit (your EPF + VPF combined), interest is tax-free. Above it, the interest on the excess is taxable.
- **One of the best safe debt options** for salaried people, often better than PPF or FDs on post-tax returns. Ask HR or payroll to start it.

## Part B: PPF (Public Provident Fund)

## 9. What PPF is
A **government-backed long-term savings scheme**, available to any **resident Indian individual**, salaried or not. Opened at **post offices** or **designated banks** (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc., often online).

## 10. Key rules
| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Interest rate** | **7.1%** (set quarterly by the government), compounded annually |
| **Minimum deposit** | ₹500 per financial year (or the account becomes "discontinued") |
| **Maximum deposit** | **₹1.5 lakh per financial year** (your own account plus minors' accounts you operate, combined) |
| **Number of deposits** | Any number of times a year, within the limit |
| **Tenure** | **15 years**, counted from the **end of the financial year** in which the account was opened (so effectively 15 years and a few months) |
| **Extension** | After maturity, extendable **indefinitely in blocks of 5 years**, with or without fresh contributions |
| **Joint account** | Not allowed. One account per person (plus accounts for minors as guardian). |
| **NRIs** | Can't open new accounts. Existing accounts can continue until maturity. |
| **Safety** | Sovereign guarantee. **Can't be attached by courts** to recover debts. |

## 11. Interest calculation trick
Interest is calculated **monthly** on the **lowest balance between the 5th and the last day** of each month, and credited at year-end.
- **Deposit before the 5th** of the month to earn interest for that month.
- **Best:** deposit the full ₹1.5 L **between 1 and 5 April**, which earns interest for the whole year.

## 12. Liquidity: loans and partial withdrawals
| Feature | When | How much |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan** | From the **3rd to the 6th** financial year | Up to 25% of the balance at the end of the 2nd year preceding the loan year. Interest = PPF rate + 1%. |
| **Partial withdrawal** | From the **7th financial year** onwards, once a year | Up to 50% of the balance at the end of the 4th preceding year or the immediately preceding year, whichever is lower |
| **Premature closure** | After **5 years**, only for specified reasons (serious illness, higher education, change of residency status) | Penalty: 1% interest reduction |

## 13. Tax: EEE
- **Deposits:** deduction under **80C** (old regime).
- **Interest:** tax-free.
- **Maturity:** tax-free.
- Even in the **new regime** (no 80C), interest and maturity remain tax-free, which makes PPF a good **tax-free debt** option for anyone.

## 14. Who should use PPF
- Self-employed people and others without EPF, who need a safe long-term debt option.
- Anyone in a **high tax bracket** wanting tax-free, sovereign debt.
- As the **debt portion of a long-term portfolio** (retirement, a child's future).
- **Tip:** even if you don't plan to invest much, **open an account early**. The 15-year clock starts from opening. Keep it alive with ₹500 a year.

### Example: ₹1.5 L every year (deposited by 5 April) for 15 years at 7.1%
- Total invested: ₹22.5 L
- Maturity value: about **₹40.7 L**, all tax-free

## Part C: NPS (National Pension System)

## 15. What NPS is
A **voluntary, market-linked, defined-contribution retirement scheme**, regulated by **PFRDA (Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority)**.
- Started in 2004 for central government employees (mandatory for new recruits) and opened to **all citizens** in 2009.
- **Eligibility:** Indian citizens (including NRIs) aged **18–70**.
- Run through **Points of Presence (PoPs)**: banks and online platforms. Easy to open via **eNPS** (the NSDL/KFintech CRA websites) or apps.
- You get a **PRAN (Permanent Retirement Account Number)**.

## 16. The two tiers
| | **Tier I** (the pension account) | **Tier II** (optional savings account) |
|---|---|---|
| Mandatory? | Yes, the main account | Optional, needs an active Tier I |
| Withdrawals | **Normal exit at 60 or after 15 years** (early exit allowed, but mostly into an annuity) | **Withdraw anytime** |
| Tax benefits | Yes | Generally none (except a special tax-saver Tier II for central government employees) |
| Minimum contribution | ₹1,000/year | ₹250 per contribution |
| Use | Retirement | Like a low-cost mutual fund. Rarely needed. |

## 17. Where your money is invested: asset classes
| Asset class | Invests in |
|---|---|
| **E: Equity** | Mainly large-cap stocks (index-like portfolios) |
| **C: Corporate debt** | Corporate bonds, PSU bonds |
| **G: Government securities** | G-Secs, SDLs |
| **A: Alternative assets** | REITs, InvITs, AIFs (max 5%) |

### Pension Fund Managers (PFMs)
You choose one PFM (you can switch once a year). Examples: **SBI Pension Funds, UTI, LIC, HDFC Pension, ICICI Prudential, Kotak, Aditya Birla Sun Life, Tata, Max Life, Axis, DSP**. Fund management charges are **extremely low** (a fraction of a percent, much cheaper than most mutual funds).

## 18. Active choice vs auto choice
### Active choice: you decide the mix
- Allocate between E, C, G and A yourself.
- **Equity cap:** up to **75%** until age 50, then reduced gradually (to 50% by 60) under the standard scheme. (PFRDA has introduced newer scheme frameworks that allow higher equity for some subscribers. Check the current options.)

### Auto choice: lifecycle funds
Equity reduces automatically as you age:
| Option | Max equity | Starts reducing equity from age |
|---|---|---|
| **LC75 (Aggressive)** | 75% | 35 |
| **LC50 (Moderate)** | 50% | 35 |
| **LC25 (Conservative)** | 25% | 35 |

Varsity's suggestion for young subscribers: use **active choice with maximum equity**, or **LC75**, since retirement is decades away.

## 19. Tax benefits
| Section (old names) | What | Regime |
|---|---|---|
| **80CCD(1)** | Your contribution, up to 10% of salary (20% of income for self-employed), **within the ₹1.5 L 80C limit** | Old only |
| **80CCD(1B)** | **Additional ₹50,000** deduction for your contribution, **over and above** 80C | Old only |
| **80CCD(2)** | **Employer's contribution**, up to **14% of basic + DA** (new regime and government employers) or 10% (old regime, private employers) | **Both regimes** |

**Key insight:** **80CCD(2) works even in the new tax regime.** If your employer offers NPS as part of your salary structure (corporate NPS), opting in reduces your taxable income without reducing your CTC. For someone in the 30% bracket, this is one of the few remaining tax breaks in the new regime.

## 20. Exit and withdrawal rules
PFRDA's December 2025 rules changed exits for **non-government subscribers** (the All Citizen and corporate models).

### Normal exit (at 60, or after 15 years of subscription)
- At least **20%** of the corpus must be used to buy an **annuity** (a pension plan from an insurer, paying monthly income for life).
- Up to **80%** can be withdrawn as a **lump sum**, which is **tax-free**.
- **Small corpus:**
  - up to **₹8 lakh**: the entire amount can be withdrawn
  - **₹8–12 lakh**: up to **₹6 lakh** can be taken as a lump sum
- You can **defer** withdrawal and stay invested until **85**.
- **Systematic lump-sum withdrawals** (phased) are allowed.

**The old rule (still applies to government employees):** at least **40%** into an annuity, at most **60%** as a lump sum.

### Before 60 (premature exit)
- The **5-year lock-in** for the All Citizen model has been **removed**.
- Corpus of **₹5 lakh or less**: can be withdrawn in full.
- Above ₹5 lakh: only **20% as a lump sum**, and **80% must buy an annuity**. That's still very restrictive, so treat NPS as money for retirement.

> These rules change often. **Check the current exit rules on pfrda.org.in before planning.**

### Partial withdrawals (before 60)
- Allowed after **3 years**, up to **25% of your own contributions**, for specific purposes (children's education or marriage, house purchase, critical illness, skill development, etc.). Limited number of times. Tax-free.

### Annuity taxation
Annuity income is **taxed at your slab rate** as income. Annuity rates (about 6–7.5%) are often unattractive after tax. This is still the main drawback of NPS, although for non-government subscribers the compulsory share is now only 20% at a normal exit.

## 21. NPS pros and cons
| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Very low cost | Meant to stay until 60 or 15 years (an early exit above ₹5 L puts 80% into an annuity) |
| Market-linked, with equity exposure | Mandatory annuity portion: at least 20% at a normal exit (taxable, low returns) |
| Extra ₹50,000 deduction (old regime) | Equity cap, limited fund choice (index-like) |
| Employer contribution deductible in **both** regimes | Rules change frequently |
| Forced discipline: can't touch it early | Not suitable for goals other than retirement |

### Related schemes
- **NPS Vatsalya** (launched 2024): an NPS account for **minors**, opened by parents and converted to a regular NPS at 18.
- **UPS (Unified Pension Scheme)**: an option introduced for central government employees from April 2025, offering an assured pension. Not relevant for private employees.
- **Atal Pension Yojana (APY)**: a guaranteed small pension (₹1,000–5,000/month) for unorganised-sector workers.

## Part D: Putting them together

## 22. Comparison
| | EPF/VPF | PPF | NPS (Tier I) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Salaried | Any resident | Any citizen, 18–70 |
| Returns | ~8.25% (declared) | 7.1% (set quarterly) | Market-linked (equity + debt mix) |
| Risk | Very low | Very low (sovereign) | Moderate (equity portion) |
| Lock-in | Until retirement; on job loss 75% at once, the rest after 12 months (partial withdrawals allowed) | 15 years (partial from year 7) | Until 60 or 15 years of subscription |
| Tax on contribution | 80C (old) | 80C (old) | 80C + extra ₹50k (old); employer 80CCD(2) (both) |
| Tax on maturity | Tax-free (5+ yrs service) | Tax-free | Lump sum (up to 80%) tax-free; annuity (at least 20%) taxable |
| Role | Core safe debt for retirement | Safe tax-free debt | Additional retirement savings with equity |

## 23. A sensible approach
1. **Salaried:** EPF happens automatically. Consider **VPF** for extra safe debt.
2. **Not salaried (or want more debt):** **PPF**, up to ₹1.5 L a year.
3. **NPS:**
   - Opt in to **employer NPS (80CCD(2))** if offered. It's valuable in both regimes.
   - In the **old regime**, consider ₹50,000/year for the **80CCD(1B)** deduction.
   - Otherwise, equity index funds give similar equity exposure **with full liquidity**.
4. Count all of these as part of your **asset allocation** (EPF/PPF/VPF = debt; NPS = split by its E/C/G mix).

---

## 24. Summary
- **EPF:** 12% + 12% of basic + DA (employer share partly to EPS). 8.25% for FY 2025-26, mostly tax-free. **Transfer on job change, don't withdraw.** On job loss, 75% can come out at once, the other 25% after 12 months, and EPS only after 36 months.
- **VPF:** voluntary extra EPF at the same rate. A top safe-debt option for salaried people.
- **PPF:** 7.1%, ₹500–₹1.5 L/year, 15-year lock-in, **EEE**. Deposit by the 5th (ideally by 5 April). Open early.
- **NPS:** low-cost, market-linked pension. Normal exit at 60 or after 15 years. **Extra ₹50k deduction (old regime)** and **employer contribution deductible in both regimes**. At exit, non-government subscribers must put at least 20% into a taxable annuity and can take up to 80% tax-free (the old 40/60 rule still applies to government employees). Check pfrda.org.in for current rules.
